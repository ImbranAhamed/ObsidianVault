Resolution refers to the clarity and detail of an image, typically measured in terms of the number of pixels displayed on a screen or captured by a digital camera or scanner. It determines the level of sharpness and fine detail that can be seen in an image.

In digital imaging, resolution is commonly expressed as the number of pixels in each dimension. For example, a resolution of 1920x1080 (commonly known as 1080p) means there are 1920 pixels horizontally and 1080 pixels vertically.

Resolution can vary depending on the device or medium:

1. **Display Resolution**: The resolution of computer monitors, televisions, and mobile devices determines the clarity and sharpness of the images and text displayed on the screen.

2. **Print Resolution**: In printing, resolution refers to the number of dots per inch (DPI) or pixels per inch (PPI) that a printer can produce on paper. Higher resolution printers can produce sharper and more detailed prints.

3. **Image Resolution**: When referring to digital images, resolution determines the level of detail and clarity captured by a digital camera or scanner. Higher resolution images contain more pixels and can be enlarged or printed at larger sizes without losing quality.


In summary, resolution plays a crucial role in determining the quality and clarity of images and text displayed on screens or printed on paper. Higher resolution typically results in sharper and more detailed images, while lower resolution may lead to pixelation and loss of detail.
